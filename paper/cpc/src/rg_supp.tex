\begin{abstract}
This supplement gives the normative specification of CON 3.1.
CON is a geometry format used in many codes, and readcon-core formalizes it.
The supplement also states the reader rules.
It then gives the test run of the release with the source lines of each step of its reader, the CON 3.1 corpus with the cross-check and the header variants, and the survey and drift files with the mechanism given to each library.
The last sections give the records beside a checkpoint with what the specification of each format states, the restart protocol, the velocity unit and the identifiers, the timing method and tables, the measured versions, and the commands that rebuild the paper.
\end{abstract}
\end{frontmatter}

We keep the specification, the corpora and the timing records in this supplement.
We name the script that writes each table.
We state a reader rule as an input and the outcome that follows from it.
We measure a wall time as the median of at least six repeats.
We place the type 7 interquartile range next to that median.
We compare each peer on the same file and the same host.
We call two reads equal when their coordinates match.
We count the frames a reader returns and the bytes it keeps.
We draw each restored figure from a record in this directory.
We leave a version deposit unnamed until the record exists.
We build the library from the public commit named in the manuscript.
We run one cell at a time on one host.
We keep the machine line to the architecture, the processor count and the processor string.

\section{Normative specification}\label{sec:spec-normative}
This section is the specification of CON 3.1 as readcon-core~\readconversion{} distributes it, the file \texttt{docs/\allowbreak orgmode/\allowbreak spec.org} of the \readconcommitrole{} \texttt{\readconcommitshort} at git blob \texttt{\specTextBlob}.
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak gen\_spec\_supp.py -{}-as-is} typesets that file, and its \texttt{-{}-check} fails unless the typeset text matches the file.
That file is byte for byte the CON 3.1 text of readcon-core~\specTextRelease{}, the first release that includes it, with its version line, its date and its \specClauses{} numbered clauses.
The library test \texttt{tests/\allowbreak ci\_gates.rs} runs \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak check\_spec\_text.sh}, which fails when the two differ.
Two informative sentences of its units section round the value the parser gives the natural time unit and its difference from the value of eOn, and they are left out here.
The parser reads \texttt{(amu*angstrom\^{}2/eV)\^{}0.5} as the binary64 number \(\velNaturalFsBinary\,\mathrm{fs}\), eOn hard-codes \(\velEonFs\,\mathrm{fs}\), and their difference relative to the parsed value, computed in binary64, is \(\velEonRelDiff\) (\texttt{bench/\allowbreak results/\allowbreak velocity\_case.json}).
The reader it specifies is readcon-core~\readconversion{}.
Each clause keeps its identifier, so a clause cited in the corpus manifest or an error message names the same text here.
Section~\ref{sec:conformance} of the manuscript measures the clauses against the reader on the conformance corpus.
The specification also points to an informative parsing expression grammar (PEG), \texttt{grammar/\allowbreak readcon.pest}, which is not reproduced.
The reader-compatibility notes and the references of the source are omitted, because they describe other programs.

\section{The CON 3.1 corpus, the cross-check and the header variants}\label{sec:supp-corpus31}
This section describes the second corpus of Section~\ref{sec:conformance}, the cross-check of both corpora and the seven headers of Table~\ref{tab:versionsafety}.
\path{paper/cpc/data/corpus31} contains the \cthreeoneFiles{} files of that corpus, which \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak make\_corpus31.py} writes byte for byte, and its \texttt{manifest.toml} names for each file the outcome, the clauses that decide it and the basis of that outcome in the released text.
\path{paper/cpc/data/corpus31-results.json} is the record of \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak corpus31\_results.py}: the outcome of every file at one, two and eight threads and the default, from the \texttt{corpus31\_report} example of readcon-core, and the build that read the files.
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak corpus\_paths.py} writes \path{paper/cpc/data/corpus_paths.json}.
It reads the \cpathsFiles{} files of both corpora through the string, file, mapped, gzip and iterator reads and the frame count of the readcon module and of the C library.
The C library is read in its default build and built with \texttt{parallel}, as the release archives build it, and the module also reads the plain and the gzip file through its stream reader, \texttt{iter\_con(stream=True)}.
Every file takes \cpathsReadKinds{} reads, except that the \cpathsNotText{} files that are not UTF-8 take no \texttt{read\_con\_string} read.
All \cpathsMatch{} of the \cpathsReads{} reads return the outcome of the manifest entry of their file, with its frame count or error class (Table~\ref{tab:corpus-paths}).
The record keeps the verdict of every read with the builds that made them.
\path{paper/cpc/data/every_path.json} is the record of \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak every\_path\_count.py}, which runs the \everyPathTestFiles{} reader-path test files of readcon-core with the \texttt{zstd} and \texttt{parallel} features, \everyPathTests{} tests in all, and counts the \everyPathInputs{} inputs that passed every path, each logged once as given and once padded.
Table~\ref{tab:corpus31-open} lists the \cthreeoneNotExplicit{} entries whose outcome no clause decides by name, each with the reading that its manifest entry records and that readcon-core follows.
Table~\ref{tab:corpus-crosscheck} is the run of \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak corpus\_crosscheck.py} over both corpora, beside the independent reader \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak indep\_reader.py}; \path{paper/cpc/data/corpus_crosscheck.json} and \path{paper/cpc/data/corpus_crosscheck.csv} record the outcome of every reader on every file.

\path{paper/cpc/data/spec/mutant_differential.json} is the record of \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak mutant\_differential.py}.
A case takes one of the \mutantFiles{} files of \path{paper/cpc/data/corpus31} under \mutantMaxBytes{} bytes and makes one to three edits at random places: it inserts a token, deletes one to eight characters or replaces one character with a token.
The tokens are whitespace and line ends, digits, signs and JSON punctuation, component headers, keys of line 2, element symbols and a whole velocity block, and the record lists them.
\texttt{random.Random} draws the file, the edits and the tokens from the seed.
Each of the \mutantSeeds{} seeds gives the same \mutantPerSeed{} cases on every run, and \texttt{-{}-check} reruns them.
The comparison covers the verdicts and the frame counts or error classes and leaves out the lines, since the exceptions of the readcon module carry none.
The record still keeps the error line of each reader.
Each group has a variant of the independent reader that follows readcon-core on one rule.
A differing case belongs to the first group whose variant returns the verdict and class of readcon-core.
In the first group, a count line has the wrong number of tokens and a token that is not an unsigned integer, so it breaks CON-error.3 and CON-num.9 on one line; CON-error.4 orders errors by line alone, and readcon-core checks the number of tokens first, the independent reader each token first.
In the second, line 2 contains a number such as \texttt{1e999}, which the JSON grammar of Request for Comments (RFC)~8259 admits and binary64 cannot represent.
The independent reader rejects such a number anywhere in line 2, and readcon-core keeps it in an unreserved key and reads on; no clause requires either reading.
In the third, the last byte of the input is a carriage return, of which CON-file.3 states nothing: readcon-core reads it as a line end, so the input ends in trailing whitespace, and the independent reader takes it for line 1 of a further frame, which ends before its line 2.
\path{paper/cpc/data/spec/clause_coverage.json}, the record of \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak clause\_coverage.py}, lists for each of the \clauseTotal{} clauses the corpus files whose manifest entries name it, the probes whose rules name it, the tests of the library whose names or documentation name it and whether the independent reader names it.
No file, probe or test names CON-count.5, a SHOULD NOT on writing for a given consumer, since no writer of readcon-core takes a consumer or a target version; CON-section.10 and CON-section.11, which govern how a later revision adds a reserved section name; and CON-section.18, a SHOULD on the energies a producer stores, whose reader side CON-section.19, accepting a frame whose energy differs from the sum of its energies rows, \texttt{tests/\allowbreak clause\_coverage.rs} reads.

The test \texttt{differential\_\allowbreak 19545\_\allowbreak agree\_\allowbreak across\_\allowbreak entry\_\allowbreak points\_\allowbreak and\_\allowbreak thread\_\allowbreak counts} of \texttt{src/\allowbreak iterators.rs} draws 19,545 cases from a fixed seed, each a test file or a conformance file of the release with one edit, such as a form feed, a no-break space, two blank lines or a further section at its end.
Each case is read from a string and from bytes at one to sixteen threads, by the stream reader at read sizes of 1 and 61 bytes and 1~MiB, and by the column reader with the positions alone and with every section, from bytes and streamed from the file.
It is then written to a plain and a gzip file, and each file is read again at the same thread counts, with the stream reader and through the indexed trajectory.
Every read returns the frame count, the coordinates and the section presence of the one-thread string read or stops with its error at the same line, and the trajectory refuses to open a file only when the string read rejects the case.
One case in every 305 is also padded with blank lines past the mapping threshold and read as a mapped file at one to sixteen threads, and each read returns the outcome of a one-thread read of the padded text.
\texttt{cargo test -{}-release -{}-lib differential\_19545} runs it.

\section{Survey frame, grading and drift files}\label{sec:supp-failure}
The survey frame of Table~\ref{tab:survey} is a Pt(100) slab of \(2\times2\times2\) atoms with 6\hspace*{.2em}\AA{} of vacuum and one Pt adatom 1.8\hspace*{.2em}\AA{} above the hollow site; Listing~\ref{lst:frame} prints it.
Every coordinate carries a uniform offset of up to \(10^{-3}\,\text{\AA}\) from a fixed seed, so writing it back takes all seventeen significant digits.
The bottom four atoms are held in every component, and the adatom is held in \(z\).
The identifiers are 17, 3, 11, 5, 23, 2, 13, 7 and 41 in row order.
Velocities are normal deviates of standard deviation \(4\times10^{-3}\) in the natural unit, zero on held components.

Each library is given each field through the mechanism its own documentation names, Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}.
A field counts as kept when it reads back equal.
A velocity is declared when the file names its unit and the values agree after conversion to within \(10^{-6}\) relative.
A velocity has ``no unit'' when it comes back from a file that names no time unit, whatever unit the reader then applies.
Read as \(\text{\AA}/\mathrm{fs}\), the natural-unit velocities of the frame give a kinetic energy \(\velFactorSq\) times too large.
One rule labels a field that came back.
It is fixed by the format when the format's own specification defines the name and meaning of the place it was stored in, and a convention when only a library's documented layout defines that place, so only readers that share the layout get it back.
A label is an identifier that came back only inside the site name of a Crystallographic Information File (CIF).
A file is named ``H5MD, private mask dataset'' when it has the H5MD root group and its version and keeps the mask in a dataset that H5MD does not define.

\subsection{Host and payloads}
The host of every timing is one Kernel-based Virtual Machine (\rcHypervisor) guest, whose processor reports itself as \texttt{\rcCpu}, of family~\rcFamily, model~\rcModel{} and stepping~\rcStepping.
The guest has \rcCores{} processing units of the \rcArch{} architecture, \(\rcMemGiB\,\mathrm{GiB}\) of memory, \rcLtwo{} of L2 cache in \rcLtwoInstances{} instances and \rcLthree{} of L3 cache in \rcLthreeInstances{} instance.
It runs \texttt{\rcOs} with Linux~\rcKernel{} and Python~\rcPython.
Every timing record carries the hardware line of that host, which is the processor model as the kernel prints it, the count of processing units and the machine architecture, and no record names the host.
The head-to-head and native-reader campaigns of Sections~\ref{sec:supp-h2h} and~\ref{sec:supp-native} ran on the same host with the same schedule.
The scripts that write their tables stop on a record that names another processor, core count, kernel or hardware line, or another seed or count of rounds.
The Python reads ran the readcon~\rcModuleVersion{} module, built from the library of the \readconcommitrole{} \texttt{\readconcommitshort} with rustc~\rcRustc.
Both readcon-core reads of the trajectory have the \texttt{parallel} feature: the Python wheel enables it, and the C++ read links the library built with it.
readcon-core wrote the trajectory as CON~\rcConVersion{} with positions and velocities, \rcTrajBytes{} bytes, and without velocities, \rcCoordsBytes{} bytes.
The peers read the same frames as extended XYZ (\rcExtxyzBytes{} bytes), the ASE trajectory, H5MD written by h5py in binary64, which h5py and MDAnalysis both read, and XYZ (\rcXyzBytes{} bytes).
Each reader returns every field of its group in Tables~\ref{tab:perf} and~\ref{tab:cpp}, keeps every frame, and releases the frames inside the timer.

\subsection{Method}\label{sec:supp-method}
We measure every timing of the paper with one schedule, \path{paper/cpc/bench/interleave.py}.
A campaign is a set of cells, and a run of a cell is one fresh process that makes its measurement once and prints it.
A campaign runs one untimed round and then \rcRounds{} timed rounds, and a round runs every cell once, in an order that one pseudo-random generator seeded with \rcSeed{} shuffles anew for each round.
A drift of the host during a campaign then falls on every cell alike instead of on the cells that run last.
The record keeps the seed, the order of every round, the runs of each cell in the order they ran, and the load averages of the host before the first round and after the last.

A Python run is a fresh \path{paper/cpc/bench/bench_child.py}, pinned to one core at one thread and to all \rcCores{} at eight, and the peers run on one thread.
It imports its reader and reads a \rcWarmFrames-frame warm-up file, and while it waits the parent reads the timed file once to load it into the page cache.
The clock runs from the signal of the parent until the reader has returned every frame and its record, and then over the release of those frames.
The release is a \texttt{del} and one pass of the garbage collector, so that frames held in reference cycles are freed inside the timer too.
The checksums of every field are computed between the two intervals, with the clock stopped and every frame alive.
A C++ run is a fresh process of \path{paper/cpc/bench/native/readcon_read.cpp} or \path{paper/cpc/bench/native/chemfiles_read.cpp}, pinned with \texttt{taskset}.
It reads its warm-up file, waits for the same signal, reads the timed file and keeps every frame and its record, then sums the checksums, frees the frames and prints the seconds from the signal to the release of the frames.
The clock does not cover the start of the process.
The Python and C++ cells form one campaign of \rcCells{} cells, and every run of a cell printed the same checksums, which match the reference values of its payload.

A time is the median of the \rcRounds{} runs of its cell, and the tables give the interquartile range (IQR) of those runs, the third quartile less the first, as \texttt{statistics.quantiles} of Python computes them with the inclusive method.
A ratio is the readcon-core median over that of the other reader.
memray~\rcMemray{} ran a second campaign of the same schedule over the Python cells.
Each capture is a fresh interpreter that warms its reader as a timing run does and reads the timed file under the tracker, with every frame still held when the tracker stops.
The peak heap of a cell is the median over its captures of the most bytes allocated inside the tracker and not yet freed, and the checksums of every capture match the reference values.
The runs of a cell are separate processes, so the run is the unit of replication \cite{kaliberaRigorousBenchmarkingReasonable2013}.
Table~\ref{tab:ordering} gives for every timing ratio of the paper the exact two-sided Mann-{}-Whitney \(p\) of the runs of its two cells \cite{mannTestWhetherOne1947}, the Hodges-{}-Lehmann estimate of the ratio with its distribution-free interval \cite{hollanderNonparametricStatisticalMethods2014}, and the Holm decision at the family-wise level \ordAlpha{} over all \ordClaims{} timing ratios \cite{holmSimpleSequentiallyRejective1979}, and Holm rejects \ordRejected{} of them.

\subsection{Column reads and the binary image}\label{sec:supp-h2h}
Two head-to-head campaigns time the column reader, which returns the positions of every frame in one array, and a binary image of those positions against readers of other files that contain the same numbers (\path{paper/cpc/bench/h2h.py}).
The coordinates campaign is \path{resources/test/cuh2.con}, the \rcAtoms-atom slab without velocities, repeated \hhFrames{} times, \hhCoordsBytes{} bytes.
The velocity campaign is a file of \hhFrames{} frames of the same slab with a velocity section in each, \hhVelBytes{} bytes, which \path{benches/make_velocity_campaign.py} writes together with an XYZ file (\hhVelXyzBytes{} bytes) and a GROMACS coordinate (GRO) file (\hhVelGroBytes{} bytes) of the same frames; \texttt{h2h.py} runs the script twice and requires the same bytes from both runs.
For each campaign, \path{benches/headtohead.py} writes the h5py dataset \texttt{position/value} in binary64 without compression or chunks (\hhHfiveBytes{} bytes), an ASE trajectory and an extended XYZ file with eight decimals, all from the first frame with the first atom moved along \(z\) by \(i \times 10^{-6}\,\text{\AA}\) in frame~\(i\).

The image of each CON file, \texttt{.rcbin}, is \hhRcbinBytes{} bytes: a header of 64 bytes with the frame and atom counts and the CRC-32C, a cyclic redundancy check (CRC), of the payload, then the positions, cell lengths and angles of every frame in binary64.

In Python, \texttt{read\_arrays} with \texttt{positions\_only} parses the coordinate rows and passes over each velocity section by the atom counts of its frame header, and with \texttt{low\_memory} it reads the file through a window instead of mapping all of it.

A run of a call is a fresh \path{paper/cpc/bench/h2h_child.py} that makes the call once untimed and then once timed, from the call until what it read is released.

\subsection{Native trajectory readers}\label{sec:supp-native}
A third campaign times readcon-core against the readers that come with the binary trajectory formats of molecular dynamics, on one system written through the writer of each format (\path{paper/cpc/bench/binary_formats.py}).
The system is \bfAtoms{} atoms on a simple cubic lattice, \bfSide{} sites to an edge at a spacing of \(\bfSpacing\,\text{\AA}\), in \bfFrames{} frames of an orthorhombic box whose three edges grow by different amounts from frame to frame.
Each atom sits at the centre of its lattice cell, moved by less than \(\bfJitter\,\text{\AA}\) in each coordinate.
No two frames are equal, since a hash of the frame, the atom and the coordinate sets each move.
The first half of the atoms are Cu and the second half H, so a CON frame, which groups its atoms by type, lists them in the order of the other formats.
One program writes the positions and box lengths in binary64 and through the writers of the formats: the GROMACS compressed trajectory (XTC) at a precision of \(\bfXtcPrecision\,\mathrm{nm}\) and the full-precision trajectory (TRR) in binary32, both by xdrfile~\bfXdrfile; the binary trajectory (DCD) of CHARMM in binary32 by the molfile code that MDAnalysis vendors at commit \texttt{\bfMolfileCommit}; and TNG by the TNG library~\bfTngVersion{} at commit \texttt{\bfTngCommit}, compressed at \(\bfTngPrecision\,\mathrm{nm}\) in one frame set.
From the binary64 values the readcon-core writer writes three CON files, in the shortest round-trip form, with six decimals and with two, and the binary image of the round-trip file.
Two decimals, \(\bfConTwoStep\,\text{\AA}\), are the \(\bfXtcPrecision\,\mathrm{nm}\) of XTC and TNG, and six decimals hold more than the binary32 of TRR and DCD.
The CON files take \bfMbCon, \bfMbConSix{} and \bfMbConTwo{}~MB, the image \bfMbRcbin{}~MB, and the XTC, TRR, DCD and TNG files \bfMbXtc, \bfMbTrr, \bfMbDcd{} and \bfMbTng{}~MB, and a second run of every writer gives the same bytes.

Every reader returns the positions and the three box lengths of every frame.
A CON atom row also carries the fixed flags and the identifier, which readcon-core parses, and the TRR file stores no velocities or forces.
Each reader is a program that times, in a fresh process, the open of the file, the read of every frame and the release of what it read.
Reading by frame, the native programs read each frame into a buffer that the next frame reuses, and the frame iterator of readcon-core builds a new frame at each step from the mapped file.
Reading all frames, every program keeps each frame in its own buffer until the read ends, and readcon-core calls \texttt{read\_all\_frames\_with\_threads}.
readcon-core also reads the positions of every frame into one array with its column reader, \texttt{read\_soa\_path}, and reads the image with \texttt{read\_rcbin}, which reads the file, checks its CRC-32C and copies the positions into a new array.
The TNG frame read makes the calls of GROMACS, the box and then the positions of each frame, and the TNG all-frames read calls \texttt{tng\_util\_pos\_read} and \texttt{tng\_util\_box\_shape\_read}.
libxtc \cite{krylovLibxtcEfficientLibrary2021} at commit \texttt{\bfLibxtcCommit} decodes the XTC file with its own kernel.
The all-frames read of readcon-core and both libxtc reads ran on one and on eight threads; the other readers take no thread count and ran on one.
GCC~\bfGcc{} built the C libraries and programs with \texttt{-O3 -DNDEBUG} and the libxtc kernel with the flags of its \texttt{setup.py}, \texttt{-march=native} and \texttt{-ffast-math} among them, and rustc~\bfRustc{} built the readcon-core program with the release profile and lock file of the library and the \texttt{parallel} feature.

Before the timing, each program wrote the positions it read as binary64 values in \(\text{\AA}\), and the script compared them with the binary64 source (Table~\ref{tab:binary-formats}).
Every reader but libxtc stays within the quantization of its file, and the round-trip CON file and the image return the source exactly.
libxtc puts \bfLibxtcOff{} atom positions, \bfLibxtcOffPercent\,\% of those in the file, beyond the tolerance of XTC, up to \(\bfLibxtcMaxError\,\text{\AA}\) from the source.
On \texttt{pep.xtc}, the test file of libxtc with \bfPepFrames{} frames of \bfPepAtoms{} atoms, libxtc returns the \texttt{pep.npy} that its own tests compare with, xdrfile returns the positions that MDAnalysis~\bfMdaVersion{} returns, and the two differ in \bfPepOff{} of the \bfPepPositions{} atom positions.
Table~\ref{tab:binary-formats} gives the libxtc times, and no ratio uses them.
On a file whose frames are in two frame sets of \bfTngSetFrames{} frames, the TNG frame read returns every frame within the tolerance and the TNG all-frames read is killed by a signal, so the timed TNG file contains one frame set.

Each cell ran unpinned in the interleaved rounds of Section~\ref{sec:supp-method}, a fresh process each run, and every run printed the frame count, atom count and checksum of the check run of its cell.
Before every run of every cell, warm-up or timed, \texttt{interleave.settle} kept every CPU busy for \(\bfSettleMs\,\mathrm{ms}\) with one pinned child process per CPU, since libxtc splits each frame evenly over its threads and a CPU that an idle period leaves slow stretches every frame.
\texttt{bench/\allowbreak settle\_period.py} measured that period on the host from a timing record of the campaign without the settle, whose SHA-256 \texttt{settle.json} keeps.
Three values come from that record: the band, the median over the \bfSettleCells{} one-thread cells of the IQR over the median, \(\bfSettleBand\,\%\); the window, the shortest median on eight threads, \(\bfSettleWindow\,\mathrm{ms}\) for libxtc reading by frame; and the gap, the longest one-thread run, \(\bfSettleGap\,\mathrm{s}\) for the TNG frame read.
Each of \bfSettleTrials{} trials idles for the gap and then keeps every CPU busy for as long, \bfSettleWindows{} windows, and each child counts its passes through its loop in every window.
The ratio of a CPU in a window is its time per pass over the median of all CPUs, and its steady value is its median ratio over the second half of the trial.
A trial settles at the start of the first window in which no ratio exceeds its steady value by more than \(\bfSettleBand\,\%\), and the period is the longest settle of the trials, that of trial~\bfSettleTrial.
A time is the median of the runs of a cell, with their IQR, and a ratio is the readcon-core median over the median of a native reader in the same read, both on one thread (Table~\ref{tab:binary-ratios}).
At the precision of each format, reading by frame, readcon-core takes \(\bfRatioRcFramesTwoXtcFrames\) times xdrfile on XTC, \(\bfRatioRcFramesSixTrrFrames\) times xdrfile on TRR, \(\bfRatioRcFramesSixDcdFrames\) times molfile on DCD and \(\bfRatioRcFramesTwoTngFrames\) times TNG.
Reading all frames, it takes \(\bfRatioRcAllTwoXtcAll\), \(\bfRatioRcAllSixTrrAll\), \(\bfRatioRcAllSixDcdAll\) and \(\bfRatioRcAllTwoTngAll\) times the same readers.
TNG reads by frame in \(\bfTTngFrames\,\mathrm{s}\) and all frames in \(\bfTTngAll\,\mathrm{s}\).
The image keeps every binary64 value and reads in \(\bfRatioRcImageXtcAll\), \(\bfRatioRcImageTrrAll\), \(\bfRatioRcImageDcdAll\) and \(\bfRatioRcImageTngAll\) times the all-frames reads of XTC, TRR, DCD and TNG.
Holm rejects every ratio of Table~\ref{tab:binary-ratios} (Table~\ref{tab:ordering}), and the bottom panel of Figure~\ref{fig:timing} draws the all-frames times behind them.
The round-trip CON file keeps every binary64 value too, and readcon-core reads it by frame in \(\bfTRcFrames\,\mathrm{s}\), against \(\bfTRcFramesSix\) and \(\bfTRcFramesTwo\,\mathrm{s}\) with six and two decimals.
On eight threads it reads all frames of the three CON files in \(\bfTRcAllEight\), \(\bfTRcAllSixEight\) and \(\bfTRcAllTwoEight\,\mathrm{s}\), against \(\bfTRcAll\), \(\bfTRcAllSix\) and \(\bfTRcAllTwo\,\mathrm{s}\) on one.

\subsection{File sizes}
Table~\ref{tab:sizes} is the byte cost of the first frame of the timed trajectory of Section~\ref{sec:performance} and of all \sizeFrames{} frames, raw and after gzip at level \sizeGzipLevel.
Every frame of that trajectory differs from the one before it, and the files of one column of the table hold the same fields.
One CON~\sizeConVersion{} frame without velocities is \(\sizeConOne\) bytes per atom, against \(\sizeExtxyzOne\) for extended XYZ with mask and identifier columns, \(\sizeAsetrajOne\) for the ASE trajectory, and \(\sizeHfiveOne\) for one H5MD frame written by h5py with binary64 positions, a mask dataset and an identifier dataset.
With velocities one CON frame is \(\sizeConOneVel\) bytes per atom.
Over \sizeFrames{} frames with velocities the raw CON cost is \(\sizeConTraj\) bytes per atom, against \(\sizeAsetrajTraj\) for the ASE trajectory and \(\sizeHfiveTraj\) for h5py.
gzip brings the CON trajectory down to \(\sizeConTrajGz\), extended XYZ to \(\sizeExtxyzTrajGz\), the ASE trajectory to \(\sizeAsetrajTrajGz\) and h5py to \(\sizeHfiveTrajGz\).
