\begin{frontmatter} \input{authors.tex} \begin{abstract} This supplement gives the normative specification of CON 3.1.
CON is a geometry format used in eOn, ASE and other codes, and CON 3.1 formalizes and extends it.
The supplement also states the reader rules.
It then gives the test run of the release with the source lines of each step of its reader, the CON 3.1 corpus with the cross-check and the header variants, and the survey and drift files with the mechanism given to each library.
The last sections give the records beside a checkpoint with what the specification of each format states, the restart protocol, the velocity unit and the identifiers, the timing method and tables, the measured versions, and the commands that rebuild the paper.
\end{abstract} \end{frontmatter}

The source of each generated table names the script that writes it, and Table~\ref{tab:versionmap} names the record behind each result.
Each reader rule gives an input and the outcome that follows from it.
We take a wall time as the median of at least six repeats and report the type 7 interquartile range.
We compare the readers on the same file and the same host, one cell at a time.
Two reads are equal when their coordinates match, and we count the frames that a reader returns and the bytes that it keeps.
We draw each restored figure from a stored record.
We build the library from the commit named in Data availability of the manuscript, and we name no version deposit before its record exists.
The machine line of a record gives only the architecture, the processor count and the processor string.

\section{Normative specification}\label{sec:spec-normative} This section is the specification of CON 3.1 of the \readconcommitrole{} \texttt{\readconcommitshort} (readcon-core~\rcMeasuredVersion{}), the specification source of that commit at git blob \texttt{\rcMeasuredSpecBlob}.
A script of the paper typesets that file, and its check mode fails unless the typeset text matches the file.
That file is byte for byte the CON 3.1 text of readcon-core~\specTextRelease{}, the first release that includes it, with its version line, its date and its \specClauses{} numbered clauses.
A continuous-integration test of the library fails when the two differ.
Two informative sentences of its units section round the value the parser gives the natural time unit and its difference from the value of eOn, and they are left out here.
The parser reads \texttt{(amu*angstrom\^{}2/eV)\^{}0.5} as the binary64 number \(\velNaturalFsBinary\,\mathrm{fs}\), eOn hard-codes \(\velEonFs\,\mathrm{fs}\), and their difference relative to the parsed value, computed in binary64, is \(\velEonRelDiff\) (Section~\ref{sec:supp-velocity}).
The reader this section specifies is the one readcon-core~\readconversion{} ships.
Each clause keeps its identifier, so a clause cited in the corpus manifest or an error message names the same text here.
Section~\ref{sec:conformance} of the manuscript measures the clauses against the reader on the conformance corpus.
The specification also points to an informative parsing expression grammar (PEG) that the library ships, which is not reproduced.
The reader-compatibility notes and the references of the source are omitted, because they describe other programs.

\FloatBarrier \section{The CON 3.1 corpus, the cross-check and the header variants}\label{sec:supp-corpus31} This section describes the second corpus of Section~\ref{sec:conformance}, the cross-check of both corpora and the seven headers of Table~\ref{tab:versionsafety}.
The records of the paper contain the \cthreeoneFiles{} files of that corpus, which a script of the paper writes byte for byte, and its manifest names for each file the outcome, the clauses that decide it and the basis of that outcome in the released text.
Its results record holds the outcome of every file at one, two and eight threads and the default, from the \texttt{corpus31\_report} example of readcon-core, and the build that read the files.
The corpus-path run reads the \cpathsFiles{} files of both corpora through the string, file, mapped, gzip and iterator reads and the frame count of the readcon module and of the C library.
The C library is read in its default build and built with \texttt{parallel}, as the release archives build it, and the module also reads the plain and the gzip file through its stream reader, \texttt{iter\_con(stream=True)}.
Every file takes \cpathsReadKinds{} reads, except that the \cpathsNotText{} files that are not UTF-8 take no \texttt{read\_con\_string} read.
All \cpathsMatch{} of the \cpathsReads{} reads return the outcome of the manifest entry of their file, with its frame count or error class (Table~\ref{tab:corpus-paths}).
The record keeps the verdict of every read with the builds that made them.
The every-path count runs the \everyPathTestFiles{} reader-path test files of readcon-core with the \texttt{zstd} and \texttt{parallel} features, \everyPathTests{} tests in all, and counts the \everyPathInputs{} inputs that passed every path, each logged once as given and once padded.
Table~\ref{tab:corpus31-open} lists the \cthreeoneNotExplicit{} entries whose outcome no clause decides by name, each with the reading that its manifest entry records and that readcon-core follows.
Table~\ref{tab:corpus-crosscheck} is the cross-check run over both corpora, beside the independent reader of Section~\ref{sec:conformance} of the manuscript; its two records keep the outcome of every reader on every file.

The mutant differential reads edited copies of corpus files with readcon-core and with the independent reader.
A case takes one of the \mutantFiles{} files of the CON 3.1 corpus under \mutantMaxBytes{} bytes and makes one to three edits at random places: it inserts a token, deletes one to eight characters or replaces one character with a token.
The tokens are whitespace and line ends, digits, signs and JSON punctuation, component headers, keys of line 2, element symbols and a whole velocity block, and the record lists them.
\texttt{random.Random} draws the file, the edits and the tokens from the seed.
Each of the \mutantSeeds{} seeds gives the same \mutantPerSeed{} cases on every run, and a check run reruns them.
The comparison covers the verdicts and the frame counts or error classes and leaves out the lines, since the exceptions of the readcon module carry none.
The record still keeps the error line of each reader.
Each group has a variant of the independent reader that follows readcon-core on one rule.
A differing case belongs to the first group whose variant returns the verdict and class of readcon-core.
In the first group, a count line has the wrong number of tokens and a token that is not an unsigned integer, so it breaks CON-error.3 and CON-num.9 on one line; CON-error.4 orders errors by line alone, and readcon-core checks the number of tokens first, the independent reader each token first.
In the second, line 2 contains a number such as \texttt{1e999}, which the JSON grammar of Request for Comments (RFC)~8259 admits and binary64 cannot represent.
The independent reader rejects such a number anywhere in line 2, and readcon-core keeps it in an unreserved key and reads on; no clause requires either reading.
In the third, the last byte of the input is a carriage return, of which CON-file.3 states nothing: readcon-core reads it as a line end, so the input ends in trailing whitespace, and the independent reader takes it for line 1 of a further frame, which ends before its line 2.
The clause-coverage record lists for each of the \clauseTotal{} clauses the corpus files whose manifest entries name it, the probes whose rules name it, the tests of the library whose names or documentation name it and whether the independent reader names it.
No file, probe or test names CON-count.5, a SHOULD NOT on writing for a given consumer, since no writer of readcon-core takes a consumer or a target version; CON-section.10 and CON-section.11, which govern how a later revision adds a reserved section name; and CON-section.18, a SHOULD on the energies a producer stores, whose reader side CON-section.19, accepting a frame whose energy differs from the sum of its energies rows, a clause-coverage test of the library reads.

\FloatBarrier \section{Survey frame, grading and drift files}\label{sec:supp-failure} The survey frame of Table~\ref{tab:survey} is a Pt(100) slab of \(2\times2\times2\) atoms with 6\hspace*{.2em}\AA{} of vacuum and one Pt adatom 1.8\hspace*{.2em}\AA{} above the hollow site; Listing~\ref{lst:frame} prints it.
Every coordinate carries a uniform offset of up to \(10^{-3}\,\text{\AA}\) from a fixed seed, so writing it back takes all seventeen significant digits.
The bottom four atoms are held in every component, and the adatom is held in \(z\).
The identifiers are 17, 3, 11, 5, 23, 2, 13, 7 and 41 in row order.
Velocities are normal deviates of standard deviation \(4\times10^{-3}\) in the natural unit, zero on held components.

Each library is given each field through the mechanism its own documentation names, Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}.
A field counts as kept when it reads back equal.
A velocity is declared when the file names its unit and the values agree after conversion to within \(10^{-6}\) relative.
A velocity has ``no unit'' when it comes back from a file that names no time unit, whatever unit the reader then applies.
Read as \(\text{\AA}/\mathrm{fs}\), the natural-unit velocities of the frame give a kinetic energy \(\velFactorSq\) times too large.
One rule labels a field that came back.
It is fixed by the format when the format's own specification defines the name and meaning of the place it was stored in, and a convention when only a library's documented layout defines that place, so only readers that share the layout get it back.
A label is an identifier that came back only inside the site name of a Crystallographic Information File (CIF).
A file is named ``H5MD, private mask dataset'' when it has the H5MD root group and its version and keeps the mask in a dataset that H5MD does not define.

\FloatBarrier \section{Records beside the checkpoint}\label{sec:supp-neighbors} Table~\ref{tab:compare} answers four questions for the checkpoint and for the other routes of Table~\ref{tab:survey}.
Restart-exact means binary64 coordinates, the per-component mask, the identifier and the periodic flags all come back.
Velocities is how the file states the velocity unit.
Metadata is the version or schema that another reader can apply.
Indexed means frame \(k\) can be opened without a scan whose length is the number of earlier frames.
Every row is a route measured in Table~\ref{tab:survey}.

The git bundle readcon-core.bundle of the supplementary material carries commit \texttt{\readconcommitshort} with its whole history, and the archive paper-cpc.tar.gz contains the paper directory with the scripts, the records and the TeX sources of both documents.

The pdfTeX build script of the listing typesets both documents with pdfTeX and BibTeX, and on \texttt{Ubuntu 24.04.5 LTS} it runs the TeX Live of the distribution, whose \texttt{pdftex -{}-version} prints \texttt{pdfTeX 3.141592653-2.6-1.40.25 (TeX Live 2023/Debian)}.

\texttt{apt install} with the arguments \texttt{texlive-\allowbreak latex-\allowbreak extra}, \texttt{texlive-\allowbreak science}, \texttt{texlive-\allowbreak publishers}, \texttt{texlive-\allowbreak plain-\allowbreak generic} and \texttt{lmodern} installs the nine with the packages they depend on.
On a host without TeX Live, the Tectonic build script named in the listing typesets both documents with Tectonic~0.17.0 on the path, three rounds of each so that the labels of each document reach the other, and stops on an undefined reference, an overfull box or a float that fits on no page.
The conda package list of the listing names the conda-forge packages of that environment with the MD5 of each, among them Python 3.12, pandoc~3.13 and the rustc of the checkout, and the pip requirements file beside it names the packages that pip adds; a script of the paper wrote both from the package records of the environment.
\texttt{pip install .} builds the readcon module from the checkout.
The regeneration script rewrites every table, figure and macro from the records without timing anything, and either build script typesets both documents.
The number trace then traces every printed number to its record and writes a ledger of the record and the rule behind each one.

The measurements are separate steps, each of which rewrites its own records.
The measure mode of the regeneration script reruns the survey, the survey listing, the worked example of Listing~\ref{lst:frame}, the drift files, the toolkits that open CON, the version-safety check, the corpus recount, the restart routes, the prefactors, the velocity case, the HCN case and the version history before it formats.
A further script runs the example program of Listing~\ref{lst:testrun} in a clone of the bundle.
Each other measurement is a named task of the pixi environment of the paper, and the description of each task says what it reruns.
The tasks rerun the eOn band, the wall times of Table~\ref{tab:restartfmt} and the sizes of Table~\ref{tab:sizes}; the timed files, the provenance record, the C++ programs and the timing, heap and Cachegrind records; the chemfiles builds, the head-to-head records and the records of the native readers; and, for the conformance records, the CON 3.1 corpus at each thread count, both corpora through the readcon module and through the default and parallel builds of the C library, the reader-path tests, the three readers of Table~\ref{tab:corpus-crosscheck}, the reader probes, the mutants of Section~\ref{sec:supp-corpus31} and the clause list.
The scripts of those tasks hold the command lines that they run for each cell, file or build, among them those of memray, Valgrind and cargo, and the environment variables that they read, with their defaults.
The timing scripts run their cells in interleaved rounds, one untimed round and then the timed rounds, each round a new shuffle of the cells from a fixed seed and each run of a cell a new process, and each timing record keeps its seed and its number of rounds.
The comparison of the earlier releases of CON 3.1 reads each of the four published wheels of Section~\ref{sec:supp-versions} in its own process and rewrites its record.
