\begin{abstract}
Saddle searches, nudged elastic bands and adaptive kinetic Monte Carlo resume from a geometry file, which is therefore the restart state of the algorithm.
A resume needs five things of that file: a mask for each atom and axis (R1), an atom identity that survives reordering (R2), velocities in a declared time unit (R3), binary64 coordinates, cell and periodicity (R4), and a versioned grammar two readers agree on (R5).
We push one masked Pt(100) slab frame through the formats in common use, and none meets all five.
A POSCAR round trip through ASE drops the adatom's height constraint, and the resumed climbing-image band ends \(70\,\mathrm{meV}\) lower.
Extended XYZ moves coordinates by \(5\times10^{-9}\,\text{\AA}\), and five readers of one extended XYZ file return four different answers.
CON 3.1 is a versioned specification of the text eOn already uses, with an executable conformance corpus and one reference implementation, readcon-core, behind a C interface for C, C++, Python, Julia and Fortran.
Resumed through CON 3.1, the band matches the uninterrupted one to \(3\times10^{-15}\,\text{\AA}\), the floor set by restarting the force field.
Reading costs \(12.6\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) per 218-atom frame.
\end{abstract}

\textit{Nature of problem:}
Rare-event campaigns stop and resume from a stored geometry.
The common formats each lose part of what a resumed search needs: a per-axis mask, a persistent atom id, the velocity time unit, binary64 coordinates, or the periodicity.
Readers of one format also disagree with each other.
The resumed search then runs on a different surface, pairs the wrong atoms, or starts from a different kinetic energy.

\textit{Solution method:}
CON 3.1 is a versioned grammar with a major and a minor number.
It states which later changes a reader of the same major must still accept, and it requires every velocity block to declare its time unit.
The default writer emits the shortest decimal that parses back to the same binary64 value.
A corpus of legal and illegal frames fixes what a conforming reader accepts.
One Rust core sits behind one C interface, and every binding calls that core, so there is a single parser.
eOn 3.6.0 reads and writes CON through it.

\textit{Additional comments including restrictions and unusual features:}
A frame stores geometry and its labels. The history of a quasi-Newton optimiser is outside it.
\end{small}

* Introduction
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:introduction
:END:
Rare events in solids and molecules set the rates of diffusion, reaction and structural change.
Harmonic transition-state theory turns a barrier and the curvatures at a minimum and a first-order saddle into a rate \cite{eyringActivatedComplexChemical1935,vineyardFrequencyFactorsIsotope1957,hanggiReactionrateTheoryFifty1990}.
The stationary points come from the nudged elastic band, its climbing-image form and the string method, and from single-ended searches such as the dimer \cite{henkelmanMethodsFindingSaddle2005,jonssonNudgedElasticBand1998,henkelmanClimbingImageNudged2000,asgeirssonNudgedElasticBand2021,smidstrupImprovedInitialGuess2014,eStringMethodStudy2002,eSimplifiedImprovedString2007,petersGrowingStringMethod2004,henkelmanDimerMethodFinding1999,heydenEfficientMethodsFinding2005,kastnerSuperlinearlyConvergingDimer2008,olsenComparisonMethodsFinding2004,walesExploringEnergyLandscapes2018,hermesAcceleratedSaddlePoint2019,hermesGeometryOptimizationSpeedup2021,hermesSellaOpensourceAutomationfriendly2022}.
When an event is too rare for a direct trajectory, hyperdynamics, other accelerated dynamics, adaptive kinetic Monte Carlo and parallel replicas reach the same landscape \cite{voterHyperdynamicsAcceleratedMolecular1997,voterExtendingTimeScale2002,mironAcceleratedMolecularDynamics2003,henkelmanLongTimeScale2001,voterIntroductionKineticMonte2007,xuAdaptiveKineticMonte2008,novotnyMonteCarloAlgorithms1995,voterParallelReplicaMethod1998,perezLongtimeDynamicsThrough2016}.
Basin hopping and minima hopping propose the next minimum \cite{walesGlobalOptimizationBasinhopping1997,goedeckerMinimaHoppingEfficient2004}.
None of these is one calculation.
A long-timescale run in eOn spawns thousands of saddle searches, stops them, and resumes them, often on another rank, another machine, or a public-resource client \cite{chillEonSoftwareLong2014,rohitgoswamiTheochemuieonV3602026,goswamiEfficientExplorationChemical2025,andersonBoincSystemPublicresource2005,pedersenDistributedImplementationAdaptive2010}.
A surrogate fitted inside the saddle loop still hands each geometry to the force evaluator \cite{goswamiEfficientImplementationGaussian2025,koistinenNudgedElasticBand2019,koistinenMinimumModeSaddle2020,petersonAccelerationSaddlepointSearches2016}.
The stored geometry is therefore not an export.
It is the restart state of the algorithm, and the next reader may be a different program in a different language \cite{pengReproducibleResearchComputational2011,inceCaseOpenComputer2012,hinsenCaringYourData2012,hinsenDealingSoftwareCollapse2019}.

The restart state has requirements that an exchange format does not.
A surface search fixes the slab and lets an adatom move only in the surface plane, so the mask is per atom and per axis \cite{jonssonSimulationSurfaceProcesses2011}.
Harmonic transition-state theory builds the prefactor from the free modes, so a mask read wrongly removes or adds a mode.
A band and a kinetic Monte Carlo event table refer to atoms by identity, which has to survive a file that groups atoms by element \cite{gundeIraShapeMatching2021,perezParallelReplicaDynamics2015,terrellDatabaseAtomisticReaction2012}.
Dynamics needs velocities in a known unit, and the natural unit of eOn, \((\mathrm{amu}\cdot\text{\AA}^{2}/\mathrm{eV})^{1/2}=10.1805\,\mathrm{fs}\), differs from a femtosecond by a factor of 103.64 in kinetic energy \cite{tiesingaCodataRecommendedValues2021,mckeeverUnitMeasurementLibraries2021,swopeComputerSimulationMethod1982,bussiCanonicalSamplingThrough2007}.
A converged band restarted from rounded coordinates is a different band.
Finally, two readers given the same bytes must agree.
The developers of MDAnalysis put that last point plainly: simulation file formats ``are often only poorly documented and just adhere to certain community expectations that can be difficult to understand for outsiders'' \cite{gowersMdanalysisPythonPackage2016}.

Chemical Markup Language and MOSAIC answered it with data models written down before the code \cite{murrayrustChemicalMarkupXml1999,hinsenMosaicDataModel2014}, and H5MD answered it for molecular dynamics output with a specification layered on HDF5 \cite{buylH5mdStructuredEfficient2014,folkOverviewHdf5Technology2011}.
It records identity, velocities and the box, and it does not define a per-axis constraint.
The Atomic Simulation Environment stores constraints as Python objects, and its trajectory file keeps them, but that file is defined by its implementation \cite{bahnObjectorientedScriptingInterface2002,larsenAtomicSimulationEnvironmenta2017}.
CON, the text eOn has written for two decades, carries a mask column and an id column, and it was never specified, so eOn and ASE read it differently \cite{rohitgoswamiTheochemuieonV3602026}.

We make that text a specification with a test suite and a single implementation.
The contributions are three.
First, a set of five requirements for a restart state, each tied to a measured failure in a common format (Section \ref{sec:requirements}).
Second, CON 3.1, a versioned grammar that meets them, and a conformance corpus that any reader can run (Sections \ref{sec:format} and \ref{sec:conformance}).
Third, readcon-core, one Rust core behind one C interface, which every binding and eOn 3.6.0 call (Section \ref{sec:implementation}).
Section \ref{sec:cases} resumes bands through each format.
Section \ref{sec:performance} reports the cost of a read and the parallel read, write and ingest.

R1 is a mask for each Cartesian axis of each atom.
R2 is an identity stored on the row, so a later frame finds the same atom after reordering.
R3 is a time unit that the file declares, so the reader does not have to guess it.
R4 is coordinates and cell that read back as the same binary64 values, with the periodic flags.
R5 is a grammar with a version, a rule for what a reader of a later minor version must accept, and a corpus that decides disputes.
The history of a quasi-Newton optimiser is not on the list.
Limited-memory quasi-Newton, the optimisers surveyed for saddle searches, and FIRE keep a short history that no geometry format stores, so a resume can match the coordinates and still take a different next step \cite{nocedalUpdatingQuasinewtonMatrices1980,sheppardOptimizationMethodsFinding2008,bitzekStructuralRelaxationMade2006}.
Section \ref{sec:limitations} returns to it.

We measure R1 to R5 on one frame in Table \ref{tab:survey}.
The frame is the Pt(100) slab used throughout: nine atoms in the bottom layer held on every axis, one adatom held in \(z\), ids that are not the row order, velocities in the natural unit, and coordinates that need all seventeen significant digits.
Each row writes the frame with one library, and we read it back with another.
ASE 3.29.0, the libAtoms extended XYZ parser 0.4.6, chemfiles 0.10.4 and MDAnalysis 2.10.0 are the peers \cite{larsenAtomicSimulationEnvironmenta2017,frauxChemfileschemfilesVersion01042023,michaudagrawalMdanalysisToolkitAnalysis2011,gowersMdanalysisPythonPackage2016}.
Section \ref{sec:supp-failure} gives the frame and the grading.

Only the CON 3.1 row keeps every field.
Extended XYZ written and read by ASE comes closest.
Its mask, its ids and its time unit come back because writer and reader share ASE's column names and ASE's internal unit, which the extended XYZ grammar does not define, so chemfiles and MDAnalysis drop them.
Its default writer prints eight decimals, and the coordinates move by up to \(4.7\times10^{-9}\,\text{\AA}\).
chemfiles writing the same frame moves them by \(4.9\times10^{-6}\,\text{\AA}\), and MDAnalysis keeps coordinates in single precision by design.
The ASE trajectory keeps the mask and the coordinates exactly, and it drops the id array; its time unit is ASE's internal unit, which the file does not name.
POSCAR selective dynamics is per-axis in the VASP definition, and VASP fixes the velocity unit \cite{kresseEfficientIterativeSchemes1996,kresseEfficiencyAbinitioTotal1996}.
The ASE writer translates the frozen slab and writes the adatom as free, so the format could carry R1 and this writer does not; POSCAR has no id and no open axis.
H5MD written by MDAnalysis declares the velocity unit, writes no mask and no id, and marks the open \(z\) axis periodic.
H5MD permits extra particle data, so a mask could be stored, but under a name that each reader would have to agree on, which is the extended XYZ situation again.
The ASE eon reader rejects a frame that carries a velocity block.
Given the coordinate block alone, it freezes the adatom on every axis, drops the ids and returns a cell with no periodic axis.
The ASE eon writer cannot express a per-axis constraint, so that route produces no file to resume from.

TNG, TRR, XTC, DCD, AMBER NetCDF and ADIOS record finished molecular dynamics runs, and none defines a constraint \cite{lundborgEfficientExtensibleFormat2014,spangbergTrajectoryNgPortable2011,abrahamGromacsHighPerformance2015,brooksCharmmBiomolecularSimulation2009,mcgibbonMdtrajModernOpen2015,rewNetcdfInterfaceScientific1990,caseAmberBiomolecularSimulation2005,brownSoftwarePortableScientific1993,godoyAdios2Adaptable2020}.
PDB, CIF, mmCIF and a LAMMPS dump name an archived atom or leave the unit outside the snapshot \cite{bermanProteinDataBank2000,hallCrystallographicInformationFile1991,westbrookStarmmcifOntologyMacromolecular2000,thompsonLammpsFlexibleSimulation2022}.
Converters keep a column when the dialect defines it, so the meaning of a mask often lives in the tool \cite{hirelAtomskToolManipulating2015,oboyleOpenBabelOpen2011,stukowskiVisualizationAnalysisAtomistic2010}.
Package-independent parsers made documented exchange the usual path \cite{oboyleCclibLibraryPackageindependent2008,verstraelenIodataPythonLibrary2021}.
A live coupling such as i-PI or the MolSSI driver interface moves coordinates during a running step, and the stopped job still resumes from a file \cite{ceriottiIpiPythonInterface2014,kapilIpi20Universal2019,litmanIpi30Flexible2024,barnesMolssiDriverInterface2021}.
TREXIO stores a wave function, a different record \cite{posenitskiyTrexioFileFormat2023}.

Line 1 is free text.
Line 2 is one JSON object with the version, the units, the periodicity and the section names \cite{brayJavascriptObjectNotation2017,langdaleParsingGigabytesJson2019}.
Lines 3 and 4 are the cell lengths and the angles in degrees.
The type count, the per-type counts and the masses follow.
Each coordinate row is \(x\), \(y\), \(z\), the mask and the id.
A blank line opens a later section.
Lengths and angles rebuild the box with \(a\) along \(x\) and \(b\) in the \(xy\) plane,
#+BEGIN_EXPORT latex
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a}=a\,\hat{\mathbf{x}},\qquad
\mathbf{b}=b\cos\gamma\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}+b\sin\gamma\,\hat{\mathbf{y}},
\end{equation}
#+END_EXPORT
after which \(\mathbf{c}\) is fixed by \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) so the cell is right-handed \cite{bekkerUnificationBoxShapes1997}.
When line 2 carries lattice vectors, those rows are the cell.
A missing periodicity flag means periodic on every axis.

\paragraph{R1, the mask}
Column 4 is the integer of Table \ref{tab:mask}.
Mask 0 is free, mask 4 fixes \(z\) only, and mask 7 fixes every axis.
A float spelling, a sign, or a value outside 0 to 7 is an illegal flag.
Under version 1 the column is 0 or 1, so masks 4 and 7 cannot be stored as written.

\paragraph{R2, the identity}
Column 5 is an integer from 0 through \(2^{64}-1\).
Writers emit ids unique within a frame.
A missing id column takes the zero-based row index.

\paragraph{R3, the time unit}
From minor 1, a velocity block must declare \texttt{units.time}, and the natural unit is spelled =(amu*angstrom^2/eV)^0.5=.
Versions 1, 2 and 3.0 are read in the natural unit, and an fs label on those files is not trusted, because producers of that era wrote natural-unit numbers under an fs label \cite{rohitgoswamiTheochemuieonV3602026}.

\paragraph{R4, the numbers}
Readers convert each finite decimal to binary64 with correct rounding \cite{ieeecomputersocietyIeeeStandardFloatingpoint2019,goldbergWhatEveryComputer1991,clingerHowReadFloating1990,clingerHowReadFloating2004}.
The default writer prints the shortest decimal that parses back to the same value, in the line of shortest printers from the early algorithms through Grisu, Errol and Ryū \cite{steeleHowPrintFloatingpoint1990,steeleHowPrintFloatingpoint2004,burgerPrintingFloatingpointNumbers1996,loitschPrintingFloatingpointNumbers2010,andryscoPrintingFloatingpointNumbers2016,adamsRyFastFloattostring2018}.
Six fixed decimals are an optional writer mode.
gzip and zstd wrap the same bytes and are recognised by content \cite{deutschGzipFileFormat1996,colletZstandardCompressionApplicationzstd2021}.

\paragraph{R5, the version}
Table \ref{tab:keys} lists the 25 reserved keys of the JSON line.
A reader accepts a later minor of a known major, keeps an unknown key on rewrite, may skip an unknown section whose width is declared, and rejects an unknown major.
H5MD uses the same split for its version pair \cite{buylH5mdStructuredEfficient2014}.
Table \ref{tab:versions} is the history, Table \ref{tab:sections} the sections reserved at minor 1, and Table \ref{tab:rules} the reader rules.
The grammar of one frame is the 50-rule listing in Section \ref{sec:appendix-abnf}, and the prose specification wins where the two disagree.

** Design rationale
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:rationale
:END:
CON stays text for three reasons.
A text checkpoint diffs under version control, so a changed mask or id shows in review.
eOn has written and read CON since its first releases, and every existing campaign is in it \cite{chillEonSoftwareLong2014,rohitgoswamiTheochemuieonV3602026}.
The eOn server and its clients exchange geometries as files, often across machines and operating systems, and text needs no agreement on byte order or library version \cite{pedersenDistributedImplementationAdaptive2010}.
The metadata is one JSON line because a JSON object takes new keys without a new grammar, carries its own version pair, and lets a reader keep a key it does not know \cite{brayJavascriptObjectNotation2017}.
Table \ref{tab:sizes} measures the cost on the 218-atom frame.
One CON frame takes 42 bytes per atom, half of extended XYZ written by ASE and less than either H5MD file, where the HDF5 structure dominates a single frame.
Over 10,000 frames H5MD falls to 24 bytes per atom in binary64 and 12.6 in single precision, against 42 for CON, and gzip narrows every gap.
H5MD is designed for that case, long trajectories where binary storage and chunked access pay off \cite{buylH5mdStructuredEfficient2014}; a checkpoint is one frame, and there text costs little.

* Conformance and drift
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:conformance
:END:
A specification is only as good as the agreement it produces between readers.
CON 3.1 writes that agreement down as data, in the way checkCIF turned the crystallographic rules into alerts a later reader reproduces \cite{spekCheckcifValidationAlerts2020,hallCrystallographicInformationFile1991}.
We publish ten legal frames and eight illegal ones as the corpus of Table \ref{tab:corpus}.
A conforming reader accepts the first group and rejects the second.

The CON 3.1 row of Table \ref{tab:survey} is the reference implementation reading its own output, which is the least a specification should guarantee.
The corpus is what lets a second, independent implementation show the same.
The Python binding and the C interface of readcon-core make the same decision on all eighteen files, and each legal file, once rewritten, rewrites again to identical bytes.
We add seven header variants of one velocity frame, aimed at R3 and R5 (Table \ref{tab:versionsafety}).
readcon-core follows the rule in each case through both entry points.
It rejects a minor-1 velocity block without a time unit, an unknown major, a missing version and a mask of 9, and it keeps an unknown key from a later minor.
The ASE eon reader, which has no version rule to follow, accepts all seven.

Extended XYZ is the strongest text alternative, with a published grammar and parsers in several languages.
Table \ref{tab:drift} gives five of its readers seven three-atom files, each legal or plausible and each one step away from a plain frame (Section \ref{sec:supp-failure}).
On the plain frame they already disagree.
chemfiles returns one coordinate a unit in the last place from the correctly rounded value, and MDAnalysis returns single precision.
The two parsers of libAtoms disagree on the mask booleans, so the same file resumes with the slab frozen through one and free through the other.
A Fortran exponent is read by the C path, read as NaN by the Python path, and rejected by the other three.
A plain comment line is a non-periodic frame to ASE and a periodic one to the C path, and the Python path rejects it.
None of these readers is wrong by the standard of its own documentation.
The grammar does not settle these cases, and no shared corpus exists to settle them.
A specification has to close that gap; the column layout is the easy part.

* Reference implementation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:implementation
:END:
readcon-core 0.17.0 is the reference reader and writer \cite{goswamiReadconcoreConconvelReaderwriter2026}.
Figure \ref{fig:architecture} is the layering.
One Rust core parses and writes, and one C interface is the waist every other caller uses.
C++ wraps it for eOn, Python binds it with array accessors \cite{harrisArrayProgrammingNumpy2020}, Julia calls it through its foreign-function interface \cite{bezansonJuliaFreshApproach2017}, and Fortran 2008 reaches the same symbols through its C interoperability.
chemfiles took the same course for trajectory formats, one C++ core with bindings in several languages \cite{frauxChemfileschemfilesVersion01042023}, and readcon-core can link it to read those formats.
The electronic-structure community argues for the same modular split, one library per concern with a stable interface \cite{oliveiraCecamElectronicStructure2020}.
Rust gives the core memory safety without a garbage collector behind a plain C interface \cite{jungRustbeltSecuringFoundations2018,perkelWhyScientistsTurning2020}.
Decimal fields go through a correctly rounded conversion on the shared path \cite{lemireNumberParsingGigabyte2021,mushtakFastNumberParsing2023}.
A behaviour difference between languages therefore requires a defect in a binding's thin accessor layer, not in a parser, because there is only one parser.

#+BEGIN_EXPORT latex
\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/generated/architecture.pdf}
\caption{Layering of the reference implementation.
The Rust core parses and writes CON.
The C interface is the waist every other caller uses, including the C++ wrapper that eOn 3.6.0 calls.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}
#+END_EXPORT

The caller owns a frame handle and frees it.
Structure-of-arrays accessors return a pointer into that frame.
Absent numeric metadata is a NaN sentinel and is not written back.

readcon-db is the campaign-storage half of the design \cite{goswamiReadcondbMmapCon2026}.
It keeps the CON text of each frame as the stored record in a memory-mapped LMDB store, with a 128-bit hash per frame for exact deduplication and indexes on atom count, composition, energy and section presence, so a query touches only the frames it selects.
Indexed random access and campaign-scale storage live there and not in the format, and the text a campaign stores is the text a resume reads.
Its MPI ingest splits the frames across ranks and inserts them in global frame order, and the stored corpus is the one the serial ingest builds.
Every binding reads and writes whole files or single frames through the same calls, and an illegal file is rejected with a named class (Section \ref{sec:supp-errors}).


#+BEGIN_EXPORT latex
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={A round-trip write in Python. The default printer preserves binary64. Six decimals are a separate argument.},label={lst:py}]
frames = readcon.read_con("slab.con")
readcon.write_con("rt.con", frames)
\end{lstlisting}
#+END_EXPORT

The reader records the byte offset where each frame starts, as an on-disk offset index does for a trajectory \cite{gowersMdanalysisPythonPackage2016}.
The scan is serial because each start depends on the counts before it.
Each frame is then parsed on its own, and the frames return in file order.
The Python binding releases the interpreter lock while the core parses and takes it back to build one object per atom.

** A band resumed through each format
We test a restart state by resuming from it.
We use an EMT Pt(100) slab of \(3\times3\times3\) atoms with its bottom layer fixed on every axis, and one adatom held in \(z\) hops between neighbouring hollows.
The endpoints were relaxed to \(0.002\,\mathrm{eV/\text{\AA}}\).
A climbing-image band of five movable images with the improved tangent \cite{henkelmanClimbingImageNudged2000,henkelmanImprovedTangentEstimate2000} is driven by fixed-step steepest descent on the projected force.
That optimiser stores no history, so a difference after a resume comes from the file and from nothing else.
Uninterrupted, the band converges to \(0.01\,\mathrm{eV/\text{\AA}}\) in 60 steps with a barrier of \(0.644561\,\mathrm{eV}\).
Each interrupted band stops at step 25, is written and read back through one route, and continues under the same rule.
Table \ref{tab:restartfmt} and Figure \ref{fig:restartfmt} are the result.

The CON 3.1 resume reads back the written coordinates bit for bit and ends within \(2.7\times10^{-15}\,\text{\AA}\) and \(6\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm{eV}\) of the uninterrupted band.
The ASE trajectory reaches the same floor.
That floor is not the file: a fresh force-field object on identical coordinates already differs at that level.
Six decimals move the band by \(6.2\times10^{-7}\,\text{\AA}\) and extended XYZ by \(4.9\times10^{-9}\,\text{\AA}\); both still converge in 60 steps.
The ASE POSCAR route is the instructive failure.
Its coordinates are exact to \(5\times10^{-17}\,\text{\AA}\), and the adatom's height constraint is gone, so the middle image gains a mode.
The band relaxes the adatom toward the surface and converges in 98 steps to a barrier \(70\,\mathrm{meV}\) lower, a different saddle on a different surface.
The ASE eon reader removes two modes, by freezing the adatom laterally, and makes the slab a finite cluster.
That band does not converge in 4000 steps.
With the periodicity put back by hand, the mask collapse alone takes 127 steps and ends \(0.042\,\text{\AA}\) away, at a barrier within \(3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{eV}\), because the straight line of this symmetric hop passes through the saddle.

** The mask and the prefactor
The symmetric hop hides what a frozen axis does to a rate.
A climbing-image band of seven images on the \(2\times2\times2\) slab was converged to \(0.01\,\mathrm{eV/\text{\AA}}\) once with the intended masks and once with the adatom frozen, as the ASE eon reader returns it, interpolated with the constraints ignored.
With the height held, the barrier is \(0.366598\,\mathrm{eV}\) on a path of \(2.90040\,\text{\AA}\), and one imaginary mode of \(-4.08\,\mathrm{meV}\) has 0.987 of its weight on the lateral motion.
The Vineyard prefactor of that constrained EMT Hessian is \(3.24\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{Hz}\) \cite{vineyardFrequencyFactorsIsotope1957}, which is separate from a measured rate of Pt self-diffusion on this face \cite{kelloggSurfaceSelfdiffusionPt0011990}.
Frozen, the barrier is \(0.366584\,\mathrm{eV}\) on \(2.95955\,\text{\AA}\), the saddle geometries differ by \(7.78\times10^{-4}\,\text{\AA}\), and there is no imaginary mode, so the prefactor is undefined.
The barrier alone would not reveal the error.
Figure \ref{fig:asemask} places both bands on the \((s, d)\) plane.

** eOn resuming its own band
The production path is eOn writing and reading CON through readcon-core.
An eOn 3.6.0 client ran the Morse Pt potential on a 28-atom slab with the bottom nine atoms at mask 7, and a band of five movable images, again under steepest descent \cite{rohitgoswamiTheochemuieonV3602026,goswamiEnhancedClimbingImage2026}.
Both the uninterrupted job and each resume take five steps and stop at that cap, so the band has not converged; the force at the last step is \(0.83265\,\mathrm{eV/\text{\AA}}\).
Both resumes reproduce the printed table of the uninterrupted job, maximum force \(3.0658\), \(1.2007\) and \(0.59762\,\mathrm{eV/\text{\AA}}\) and highest image \(0.8305\), \(0.6911\) and \(0.6417\,\mathrm{eV}\).
After five steps the round-trip barrier is \(0.63493\,\mathrm{eV}\), identical to the uninterrupted band, with a largest minimum-image displacement of \(8.9\times10^{-15}\,\text{\AA}\).
Six decimals shift the barrier by \(+1.0\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{eV}\) and the images by up to \(4.9\times10^{-7}\,\text{\AA}\).
Figure \ref{fig:restart} shows the three bands, and Section \ref{sec:supp-restart} the per-image displacement at each step.
An L-BFGS resume, eOn's default band optimiser, ends at \(0.6707\,\mathrm{eV}\) against \(0.6336\,\mathrm{eV}\) for both printers, because the history is not in either file.
A dimer from the round-trip geometry reproduces the uninterrupted saddle energy, \(-112.618941\,\mathrm{eV}\), and curvature, \(-10.613681\), and six decimals shift that energy by \(-3.0\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{eV}\) \cite{goswamiEfficientExplorationChemical2025}.

** The velocity unit and identity
On the 28-atom slab the kinetic energy is \(0.888\,\mathrm{eV}\) in the natural unit and \(92.02\,\mathrm{eV}\) if the same numbers are read as \(\text{\AA}/\mathrm{fs}\), a factor of 103.64.
One velocity Verlet step of \(1\,\mathrm{fs}\) then misplaces atoms by an RMSD of \(0.096\,\text{\AA}\) (Section \ref{sec:supp-velocity}).
For identity, the public HCN and HNC endpoints were written with ids 2, 0 and 1 and an \(x\)-only mask on hydrogen \cite{vanmourikAbInitioGlobal2001,goswamiReproducibleOrchestrationBest2026}.
readcon-core returns that mask, those ids and the declared unit.
The ASE eon reader does not accept a velocity block and, on the coordinate block, reports hydrogen frozen on every axis with no id (Section \ref{sec:supp-fidelity}).

** Cost of a read
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:perf-read
:END:
A resume reads its checkpoint once per force call at most, so a read has to stay cheap beside that call.
The timings use one file on one Intel Xeon processor of family 6, model 207, with 8 hardware threads, rustc 1.88, g++ 13.3 and Python 3.12.
The file is \(167.71\,\mathrm{MB}\): 10,000 copies of one 218-atom frame, read from a warm page cache.
Repeating one frame removes frame-to-frame variation, so the rate is the cost of this frame; production trajectories will differ.

** Parallel read, write and ingest
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:perf-mpi
:END:
The output of every parallel path is independent of the rank count: each file read or written has the sha256 of the serial one, and each ingested store has the canonical dump of the serial ingest, on every rank count measured.
The MPI paths take the caller's communicator and give each rank a contiguous block of frames, so the rank-ordered concatenation is the serial read.
On a \(1.07\,\mathrm{GB}\) file of 3,167,382 frames, with MPICH 4.2.3 and one thread per rank, a read with every rank mapping a shared file falls from \(9.39\,\mathrm{s}\) on one rank to \(1.63\,\mathrm{s}\) on eight, a speedup of 5.74, and rank 0 scattering byte ranges reaches 4.71 (Figure \ref{fig:mpi}a).
The collective write places each rank's bytes at an exclusive-scan offset; through MPI-IO it falls from \(7.94\,\mathrm{s}\) to \(1.22\,\mathrm{s}\), a speedup of 6.52, and gathered to rank 0 it reaches 4.25.
readcon-db \cite{goswamiReadcondbMmapCon2026} ingests four copies of a \(1.07\,\mathrm{GB}\) trajectory, batch 4096, in Table \ref{tab:dbmpi} and Figure \ref{fig:mpi}b.
With one writer the ingest falls from \(30.6\,\mathrm{s}\) on one rank to \(15.3\,\mathrm{s}\) on eight, a speedup of 2.00, and with four shards from \(32.5\,\mathrm{s}\) to \(17.1\,\mathrm{s}\), a speedup of 1.90.
At 16 ranks the two modes take \(35.2\,\mathrm{s}\) and \(43.5\,\mathrm{s}\); the serial ingest on the same host takes \(22.8\) to \(25.9\,\mathrm{s}\) up to eight ranks and \(51.6\,\mathrm{s}\) at 16.

* Limitations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:limitations
:END:
CON 3.1 is a text format for the frames a search checkpoints, not for analysis trajectories.
Text costs space and a parse (Table \ref{tab:sizes}), which the core pays at \(1.3\,\mathrm{GB/s}\).
Indexed random access and campaign storage are readcon-db's part of the design \cite{goswamiReadcondbMmapCon2026}.
A frame specifies geometry and its labels, not optimiser state, so an L-BFGS resume restarts its history; a key for that history is the natural next minor version.
The conformance corpus fixes what a conforming reader decides on each listed file, and it says nothing about a reader on files outside it.
A single core behind every binding makes the bindings agree by construction, which is a property of this implementation and not evidence about a second one.
The resume studies are one symmetric EMT hop and one Morse band; they show what a lost field does to a saddle, and they are not a survey of reactions.
The peer comparisons hold for the library versions named in Section \ref{sec:requirements}.

* Conclusions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:conclusions
:END:
A geometry file in a rare-event campaign is the restart state of the search, and it needs a per-axis mask, a persistent id, a declared time unit, binary64 coordinates with their periodicity, and a version two readers agree on.
On one masked slab frame, no common format keeps all five, and the losses change the computed saddle: a POSCAR resume ends \(70\,\mathrm{meV}\) lower, and an ASE eon resume loses two modes and the periodicity.
Five readers of one extended XYZ file give four answers.
CON 3.1 specifies the text eOn already writes, its corpus decides what a reader must accept, and readcon-core gives every language the same parser.
A band resumed through it matches the uninterrupted band to the floor set by restarting the force field, and a frame costs \(12.6\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) to read.
